Non-destructive editing

Adjustments are never written into the original file. They are stored in a separate SQLite database and applied on the fly, both on screen and on export.

Tonal corrections

CorrectionRangeDescription
Brightness−1.00 to +1.00Lightens or darkens the whole image
Contrast−1.00 to +1.00Widens or narrows the tonal range
Saturation−1.00 to +1.00Strengthens or weakens the colours (−1 = B&W)
Gamma0.10 to 3.00Brightness curve (1.0 = neutral)

Colours (Black & White with channel mixing)

  • Tick Black & White and dose the Red/Green/Blue contributions (−1 to +1).
  • Example: Red +1, Blue −1 → dark sky and light skin tones (the film-era red filter).

Vignette

  • Adds a progressive darkening (or lightening) around the edges of the image.
  • Strength — from 0.00 (no effect) to 1.00 (maximum effect).
  • Colour — Black (dark fade, film look) or White (light fade, retro look).
  • The shape and position of the vignette are adjusted directly on the photo:
HandleEffect
Inner circle (dotted)Start of the fade
Outer circleEnd of the fade
Round handle at the topRotate the ellipse
Cross in the middleMove the centre of the vignette

Geometry

  • ↺ / ↻ — rotate 90° anticlockwise / clockwise.
  • Straighten — corrects the tilt of the horizon (−10° to +10°), with a reference grid.
  • Crop — free selection, or the 10×15 / 13×18 formats (portrait or landscape). The crop area is remembered between sessions.
  • Mirror H / V — horizontal or vertical flip.

Red eyes

  • Click Red eyes, then click each affected eye directly on the photo.
  • Size — radius of the correction (0.5 % to 8 % of the smallest dimension of the image).
  • Clear all — removes every correction placed. Done (or Esc) — leaves the mode without losing anything.

Decorative frame

The Frame button opens a gallery of previews of the current photo, one per pattern. The frame is added around the photo: it covers no pixel of the image, which simply comes out larger on export. Two exceptions, described below: the foliage surrounds, a few motifs of which bite into the edge of the image, and the second frame of the flat surround.

  • Thickness (or Outer frame), Gap and Inner frame are set as a percentage of the short side of the photo: the same setting looks the same on a thumbnail and on a full-resolution export.
  • Carved patterns need some thickness to read: picking one of them automatically raises the thickness if it is too small. Each thumbnail in the gallery shows exactly the frame you get by clicking it.
  • Fill style (solid, gradient, glitter) and second colour: reserved for the Simple and Double patterns.

Foliage surrounds that spill over

On Vine leaves, Roses and Flowers, a few shoots, roses or bunches pass over the edge of the photo, with their drop shadow, as on a carved frame that bites into the canvas. They are deliberately irregular — a spill at regular intervals would turn back into a frieze — and stay attached to the band: the centre of the image is never touched. Nothing to set: the pattern brings it. Cropping, red eyes, annotations and faces keep working on the whole photo.

Second frame of the flat surround

For the Flat surround only, tick Second frame over the photo: a line is drawn onto the photo, away from the edge — the strip of image left visible between the two frames is precisely the intended effect. Gap sets that distance, Second frame its thickness.

This line can take the shape of a piece of ironwork:

IronworkEffect
Plain lineA clean stroke (the default choice)
Corner scrollsForged coils in the four corners
Running scrollworkCorner scrolls + motifs repeated along the sides
Twisted barTwisted bar, finials at the corners and in the middle of the sides
Forged studsA row of round-headed studs, larger at the corners
  • Rendering — Light relief (light and shade on the metal) or Flat colour (solid silhouette, more graphic). No effect on the plain line, always flat.
  • Ornaments — size of the motifs, from 40 % to 250 %. The ornaments grow towards the inside of the photo: they never spill onto the outer frame.

The gallery previews update with your settings, and the viewer shows the result live. Nothing is saved until you have clicked Apply — Cancel closes the dialog without changing anything.

Annotations

A non-destructive drawing and text layer, laid over the photo — independent of the tonal corrections and of the geometry.

  • Tools — pen (freehand), line, curve (click the waypoints, double-click to confirm), rectangle, ellipse, text, and selection (to change or move an item already placed).
  • Style — stroke/text colour, thickness, fill colour and opacity of the shapes, blur of the photo under the shape, font/size/bold/italic for text.
  • Double-click an existing text (Selection tool): reopens the editor in place.
  • Delete the selection — removes the selected item. Clear annotations — removes the whole layer from the photo.
  • ✏ Annotations button at the top of the window (next to EXIF) — shows/hides the layer without deleting anything; this setting is not saved, it only lasts for the session.

Annotations are included in the export and when saving the processed image, like the other edits — unless the layer is hidden with the ✏ Annotations button at the time of the export.

Workflow

  • Only one tool active at a time — selecting a new tool (Crop, Red eyes, Annotations, Brightness/Contrast/Colours/Vignette/Straighten…) automatically confirms the work in progress in the previous tool, then closes it.
  • Double-click a slider: resets the value to zero.
  • The preview updates in real time (≤ 60 ms).
  • There is no “Save” button: every edit you confirm is written to the edit database straight away, and you will find it unchanged next time you open the photo. The original file on disk, however, is never modified.
  • Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y — undo/redo, persistent between sessions (50 states max per photo).
  • Reset every edit — removes at once every edit and the history of the photo (without confirmation), to start again from the original. The file on disk is never modified.
  • Restore every edit — puts back the edits removed by the last Reset on this photo. This button is disabled as soon as a new edit is applied after the reset (the snapshot is no longer valid).

Writing the edits into a file

The edits live in a separate database: the original file is never touched. To get a really edited version on disk, there are two distinct routes.

Save the edited image  (Ctrl+S, or right-click the photo)

One photo at a time, at its original resolution. The dialog offers two options:

  • Overwrite the original file — irreversible. A checkbox, ticked by default, first copies the original into the hidden .tmp_originals folder next to the file.
  • Save to another location… — opens the file browser, with a name already suggested, suffixed _edited.

Export  (⬆ Export button, at the top of the window)

Exports into a destination folder: the photo shown in the viewer, or every photo selected in the grid. This is where — and only where — the four size presets are offered:

PresetMax. resolutionEstimated weightTypical use
Maximum sizeOriginal resolutionvariable Archiving, large-format printing, editing in another program
Large (~4 Mpx)≈ 2,600 × 1,500 px600–1,600 KB A4/A3 printing, high-quality sharing
Medium (~2 Mpx)≈ 1,800 × 1,100 px320–800 KB On-screen display, slideshows, light e-mail attachment
Small (~500 kpx)≈ 900 × 560 px75–300 KB Social networks, web preview, mobile messaging

Tip: To send a photo by e-mail or post it on a social network, prefer Medium or Small — a photo at maximum size can exceed 5 MB and be refused, or slow the upload down. The original on disk is never modified.

EXIF

  • I key (or the [i] button): shows/hides the EXIF panel in the viewer.
  • Tools › Sync creation dates with EXIF — corrects the dates in bulk.
